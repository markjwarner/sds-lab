Catherine (Cathy) O'Brien is a Research Fellow in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) involved in the collection, analysis, and dissemination of qualitative research for two research projects in the Information Security Group at the Department of Computer Science:
 
Towards a Smart Digital Forensic Advisor to Support First Responders with At-Scene Triage of Digital Evidence Across Crime Types
A UKRI funded project exploring the triage of digital devices by first responders at scene in cases of homicide and stalking and harassment, laying the foundations for the development of a smart digital forensic advisor tool that will assist with informed decision making at scene. Methods: semi-structured interview (online and in-person), questionnaire, observation, co-design workshops using evidence-based design artefacts.
 
UseCRT - Usability and User Experience of Online Content Reporting Tools
A REPHRAIN funded project evaluating the user experience and usability of user content reporting tools in the most popular social media platforms and their user-to-user messaging services; investigated within the context of online harassment as defined by the platforms themselves. Methods: policy review through inductive and deductive content analysis.
 
She holds a DPhil in Archaeology (cognitive and anthropological) from the University of Oxford, and her thesis was titled: Potting in the Pandemic: Investigating pottery skill development and its wellbeing impacts in Covid-19 Britain. This work employed digital and sensory ethnographic methods to investigate how people were learning pottery skills and building craft communities using digital and social media during the pandemic, and the wellbeing impacts of this. Methods: digital ethnography, sensory ethnography, semi-structured interviews, visual methods (photography, sketching, video, and elicitation).
