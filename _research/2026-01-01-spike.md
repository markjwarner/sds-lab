Social engineering, a key tool for cyber-attacks, manipulates individuals into compromising personal information often by mimicking trusted contacts or creating persuasive scenarios and constitutes a staggering 98% of all cybersecurity breaches. In the UK, over 7.78 million cyberattacks happened making the country the second most affected globally. These attacks have profound implications for mental health, exacerbating social stress and creating significant emotional distress and severely traumatic experiences for victims. The increasing sophistication of artificial intelligence (AI) exacerbates this issue. AI-driven attacks can simulate individuals with striking realism, making it increasingly difficult to differentiate between legitimate and malicious interactions. While existing research has extensively explored the general population's vulnerability to cyberattacks, it has largely overlooked the role of mental health needs, leaving critical gaps in understanding the susceptibility to digital threats. Social anxious individuals are potentially more vulnerable to social engineering attacks due to a preference for online communication, biased perception of AI reliability, and difficulties in detecting deceptions. With the rapid advancement of AI technologies, these risks are poised to grow, necessitating a deeper understanding of how social anxiety intersects with cybersecurity vulnerabilities. 

<p>Social Phobia Interventions for Cybersecurity Exploit Mitigation (SPIKE) seeks to address this pressing need by combining interdisciplinary expertise and innovative research methodologies to understand better and mitigate these risks. </p>

# The project aims to: 

- Understand the Role of Social Anxiety in Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities: We will explore their cognitive and emotional responses to co-created social engineering scenarios through co-designed interview studies with socially anxious individuals. This will help identify factors contributing to susceptibility and pinpoint key areas for intervention. 
- Co-Design Solutions with People with Lived Experience: A co-design workshop will bring together individuals with lived experience, cybersecurity experts, and clinicians to collaboratively develop a research agenda for the next iteration of this project. This approach ensures that the solutions we propose are grounded in real-world experiences and helps craft solutions supporting everyone in our digital society. 
- Share Findings: Publish results in top-tier HCI and psychology journals and create documents for cybersecurity technology experts. We will organise a webinar at the end to share our learnings with key stakeholders. 
- Foundation for Future Work: Seek funding to prototype and evaluate interventions to increase the resilience of socially anxious individuals against social engineering attacks.
