Societies rely on information to function. Our political systems assume that people are being provided with reliable and trusted information to make informed decisions. Public health relies on people having access trusted and reliable information sources to help them make good decisions related to their health and wellbeing. With more people using online platforms for information-seeking (e.g., news, politics), and for making sense of their lives (e.g., health issues), information integrity is becoming more challenging. Misinformation is easily created and spread, and this is becoming ever more challenging with the advent of large-language models. This theme focuses on the question: How can we build more robust online information ecosystems in an area of large-scale misinformation spread?
